# Addendum — 22 November 2026

Everything in `research/`, `proposal/`, `method/` and `review/` was written on **28–29 August 2026**, two and three days after the disaster, and is preserved unaltered. This addendum records what has changed since, so that nothing in this repository is mistaken for a current account.

**Read this before citing any figure from the research files.**

## The casualty figures in the research are badly out of date

The research recorded the toll as of 15:00 NPT on 28 August 2026: **538 dead, 977 missing.** Those were explicitly provisional and labelled as such throughout. They have been superseded many times over. The table below comes from a later consolidated timeline, which puts the 28 August figure higher than the snapshot the research used.

| As of | Dead (Nepal) | Missing (Nepal) |
|---|---|---|
| 26 Aug 2026 | ~360 | ~1,400 |
| 28 Aug 2026 (later consolidated figure) | ~579 | ~1,924 |
| 1 Sep 2026 | >1,000 | — |
| 5 Sep 2026 | 1,355 | ~5,000 |
| 12 Sep 2026 | 1,386 | 5,130 |
| 30 Sep 2026 | 1,454 | 4,718 |
| **5 Oct 2026 (search and rescue ended)** | **at least 1,455** | **5,285** |

Tibet reported 43 dead and 519 missing as of 16 September 2026. Combined, the event killed on the order of **1,500 people with roughly 5,800 unaccounted for** — close to three times the deaths and five times the missing that the research was written against.

Any per-district breakdown in the research files, including the Chitwan and Nawalparasi figures that Run 3 used to probe where people actually died, is a 28 August snapshot and should not be treated as the final distribution.

*Source: Wikipedia, "2026 Nepal–Tibet floods," consolidated timeline of official NDRRMA and Nepal Police figures, retrieved 22 November 2026. Figures trace to NDRRMA situation reports; verify against the primary reports before citing.*

## What has since been settled

**The cause is no longer disputed.** The research treated the earthquake-versus-collapse question as open and the source slope's side of the border as contested. The established account is a large rock-ice avalanche from the **Lirung massif** that became a highly mobile, long-runout debris flow, with the Ms 5.2 seismic signal **generated by the collapse rather than preceding it** — confirmed by USGS through long-period waveform analysis and satellite imagery. Run 1's hypothesis H2 was correct.

**The warning-failure diagnosis has been independently confirmed.** Later accounts state that a China–Nepal flash-flood early warning system had been maintained for years but did not detect this flood in time, because the water-level monitors were configured for monsoon and perennial flooding, and the upstream Nepali stations were swept away before alerts could be sent. Four flood monitoring stations were destroyed. That is the central finding of Run 1 and Run 2, reached independently here from the gauge record.

## Institutional developments that change the build case

Two things created in September 2026 did not exist when this research was written and materially affect the recommendations in `review/review-adjudication.md`:

1. **A seven-member expert panel** convened by Nepal's Ministry of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation, covering hydrology, glaciology, geology, remote sensing, hydropower infrastructure and disaster risk reduction, to investigate the cause scientifically. This is now the most relevant official body, and probably the correct recipient for any synthesis.
2. **The "Kathmandu 10 Action Points,"** released by NDRRMA with ICIMOD, on upgrading disaster preparedness, early warning, and emergency response and recovery. **This should be read before any further design work.** It may already commit to what the build brief proposes, which would answer the duplication question in §4 and §7 of the adjudication.

There is also at least one academic preprint on the event whose title states the conclusion this research reached independently — that warning latency exceeded flood travel time. Locate and read it before claiming novelty.

## What this means for the repository

The research stands as a **time-stamped analytical artifact**: a record of what could be established from public sources within 72 hours of a disaster, and of a method for doing that. It does not stand as a current account of the event, and the build recommendations were written before the official panel and the Kathmandu 10 Action Points existed.

Nothing in `proposal/` was built as specified, and `proposal/watcher_prototype.py` has never been validated. The gauge monitor at the root of this repository was built later, in November 2026, and is deliberately narrower than the build brief: it watches DHM's public feed and sends no alerts. No institution has been contacted.
