# The Bhote Koshi Warning Clock

## Reconstructing 26 August 2026 and 8 July 2025, and asking when a warning could have existed

**Report date: 28 August 2026. The primary event is two days old. Every casualty figure below carries an as-of stamp, and several key timings are in active dispute between sources. Nothing here should be treated as settled.**

---

## 1. Executive summary for the go/no-go decision

Three findings dominate.

**First: detection was never the binding constraint at the top of the valley — classification and dissemination were.** The collapse generated a seismic signal at 08:37 Nepal time (02:52 UTC) that was recorded and catalogued globally within minutes. The United States Geological Survey initially published it as a magnitude 4.4 *earthquake* (event us7000tbwb) and only later reclassified it as a magnitude 5.2 *landslide* source. The physical signal arrived on time. The interpretation did not.

**Second: Nepal's automated hydrological network did not merely fail — it failed in a specific, instructive way.** Every gauge in the upper corridor was destroyed *below* its warning threshold. Rasuwagadhi's last reading was 1.62 m against a 6 m warning level. Syaphrubesi's was 3.80 m against 5.5 m. Betrawati's was 3.55 m against 4.1 m. The first automated threshold crossing anywhere in the system came at 11:20–11:26 at Furke/Malekhu, roughly 100 km downstream and nearly three hours late. A threshold-crossing architecture is structurally incapable of warning about this hazard class, because the flood destroys the sensor before the sensor crosses the threshold. **The station's death is the signal, and no one was listening for it.**

**Third, and most uncomfortable for the "more lead time saves lives" thesis: most recorded deaths occurred in districts that received hours of warning.** As of 15:00 NPT on 28 August 2026, Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority reported 538 dead, of whom 221 were in Chitwan and 134 in Nawalparasi East — both far downstream, both warned by SMS from roughly 09:16, both hours ahead of the wave. Rasuwa, the district that was destroyed with zero warning, records 12. **This distribution may reflect where bodies were recovered rather than where people died** — a debris flood carries victims tens of kilometres — and I cannot resolve that ambiguity from available sources. It is the single highest-value question for anyone deciding whether to fund this capability, and it should be resolved before capital is committed.

**Recommendation shape:** GO for a valley-specific upstream tripwire plus siren architecture in the Bhote Koshi/Lhende corridor, where the July 2025 event had already told everyone where to put it. NO-GO for generic satellite-precursor prediction of ice-rock avalanche timing, which the Chamoli literature has tested and rejected. Conditional GO for seismic source-discrimination, whose value depends entirely on solving the landslide-versus-earthquake classification problem inside two minutes.

### The multi-pulse sequence and the re-entry problem

At least three distinct hazard episodes, and the third is the most instructive.

1. **T0, 08:37** — the primary collapse, M5.2.
2. **T+3h, ~11:37** — a second mass movement, M4.2, per USGS. Downstream consequences not documented. This occurred while rescue was mobilising.
3. **28 August** — a barrier lake formed near the confluence of the Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo (Lhende system, Tibetan side) breached or began overflowing. China's Ministry of Water Resources estimated 1.5–2 million m³ as of 27 August, a further 3 million m³ expected over three days. Chinese authorities warned on 27 August that the lake was overflowing and at high breach risk, ran flood simulations, and deployed an eight-member engineering team for aerial survey and 3D modelling. Rasuwa Chief District Officer Narendra Pariyar was notified by the Nepali Army on 28 August: "We can see that the level of water in the river is rising." Rescue operations reported halted (The Week, 28 Aug 2026). Pawan Bhattarai: "even moderate overflow can erode the barrier and trigger a sudden, destructive outburst flood downstream."

**The contrast is the finding.** For the primary event, cross-border warning lead time was *negative*. For the secondary barrier-lake threat, it was roughly *24 hours* — because a lake is a persistent object that can be imaged, modelled and communicated, whereas a slope collapse is an instantaneous event. **Any warning architecture for this hazard class will perform very differently on the two sub-hazards, and a business case that averages them is misleading.** Post-event impoundments are a tractable, high-lead-time problem. Primary collapses are not.

This also defines the re-entry problem concretely: rescue teams, journalists and returning residents entered the corridor on 27–28 August into a valley containing at least one unstable impoundment upstream, in a foreign jurisdiction, with no in-channel instrumentation. Any deployed system should treat *post-event* monitoring as a first-class requirement, not an afterthought.

The deceleration is the whole story: roughly 85 km/h in the confined upper gorge, decaying to a few km/h across the lower floodplain. **Lead time is distributed catastrophically unevenly — the upper valley has minutes; the lower valley has hours. These are two different warning products with two different technologies, and conflating them is the standard failure mode of GLOF early-warning proposals.**

### Was there an observable flow drop while the dam impounded?

**Not observed, and not resolvable with the available record.** A significant negative finding.

The theoretical attraction of a pre-breach flow drop: it is the only signal in the entire cascade that arrives *before* the destructive wave. If a river above a settlement suddenly stops flowing, that is an unambiguous, physically distinctive, easily automated alarm.

What the record shows: Rasuwagadhi's final reading of 1.62 m against a 6 m warning level is *low*, but no reported time series shows a *decline*. Three obstacles prevent resolution:

1. **Sampling interval unknown.** DHM telemetered reporting intervals for these gauges are not published in any source reached. If the interval is 15 minutes and the impoundment lasted 7–20 minutes, the drop is unresolvable in principle.
2. **Wrong location.** The impoundment was ~20 km upstream of the nearest gauge, inside Tibet.
3. **The impoundment duration itself is disputed.** The one quantitative figure — "roughly twenty minutes" (South Asia Monitor) — is inconsistent with a surge reaching Gyirong at T+7 and killing the Rasuwagadhi gauge by T+13.

**Implication:** the flow-drop tripwire is attractive but unvalidated for this event. Treat it as a *hypothesis to be tested by deployment* — a high-rate (≤60 s) gauge immediately below the confluence would settle it. **Do not build a business case on it.**

## 5. Mechanism: what is settled and what is not

**Settled (confirmed, multi-source):**
- The flood was generated by a rock/ice avalanche that entered the Lhende Khola, dammed it, and produced a dam-break surge. Not rainfall. Not, per Petley, a classical GLOF.
- The seismic signal was produced *by* the mass movement, not by a tectonic earthquake that triggered it. USGS reversed its own initial interpretation.
- The water came from ice converted to melt by collapse energy, water held in entrained sediment, and river water impounded and released (Petley).

**Provisional or unresolved:**
- **Volume.** Not constrained.
- **Which side of the border the source slope sits on.** USGS: north side of Langtang Lirung. Kayastha: Nepal side. Much reporting: Tibet. This determines which state's monitoring obligation applies.
- **Impoundment duration.** Disputed; the one published figure is internally inconsistent.
- **Breach mode** (overtopping vs piping). Unresolved.
- **The role of the second M4.2 event** in the downstream hydrograph. Unknown.
- **Climate attribution.** Kristen Cook (Université Grenoble Alpes): "While we cannot at this point directly link this collapse to climate change, it is not unexpected to see more events of this type with a warming climate." Alton Byers (CU Boulder / INSTAAR): "All the indicators, to me, point to the impacts of warming trends." Statements about *base rates*, not this event.

## 6. Precursors, labelled

The OPERATIONAL bar: could a realistic system have emitted *"slope velocity exceeded X for Y hours; collapse probability elevated"* **before 08:37**, at stated confidence?

| Claimed precursor | Label | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Warm temperatures / reduced snow cover before the event | **RETROSPECTIVE** | Single low-grade source; attribution "remains unestablished." Seasonal-scale conditioning variable, not a timing signal. |
| Slope deformation / creep at the source | **NOT DEMONSTRATED — no evidence either way** | No pre-collapse deformation reported. Planet imagery brackets 25–26 August (Petley, citing Dan Shugar), so pre-event imagery *exists*, but nothing anomalous published two days on. |
| Crevassing / visible cracking | **RETROSPECTIVE at best** | Not reported pre-event. AP (27 Aug 2026): no precursors identified as visible in advance. |
| Meltwater ponding at the source | **NOT APPLICABLE** | Petley: "There is no evidence that this was a GLOF." No source lake identified for the primary event. |
| **Prior activity in the same valley — the 8 July 2025 outburst** | **ACTIONABLE (at watershed scale)** | The only precursor that clears the bar, and it clears it decisively. It does not predict *when*. It identifies *where* with near-certainty, thirteen months in advance. |
| Permafrost degradation | **RETROSPECTIVE / base-rate** | Raises regional prior probability. No timing. |

**Bottom line: nothing that could have timed this collapse was available, and on current evidence nothing existed to be available.** The signal that mattered was not about the slope at all — it was that this exact 16 km corridor had been destroyed by a transboundary cryospheric flood thirteen months earlier and had been formally flagged as continuing high risk.

## 7. Chamoli, 7 February 2021 — the decisive negative test

**Shugar et al., *A massive rock and ice avalanche caused the 2021 disaster at Chamoli, Indian Himalaya*, Science, 2021:** ~27 million m³ of rock and glacier ice, transforming into an exceptionally mobile debris flow.

**Van Wyk de Vries et al., *Pre-collapse motion of the February 2021 Chamoli rock–ice avalanche*, NHESS 22:3309–3327, 2022** — the single most important paper for this decision:

- The 26.9 × 10⁶ m³ block moved **over 10 m in the 5 years preceding collapse**, at 5–20 m/yr (Sentinel-2 feature tracking: 155 images, 5,237 pairs; Sentinel-1 InSAR: 108 interferograms; multi-sensor DEMs).
- **"Despite observing a clear precursory signal, we find that the timing of the Chamoli rock–ice avalanche could likely not have been forecast from satellite data alone."**
- The largest changes **preceded failure by around three years**. No terminal acceleration.
- Not a data-gap artefact — cloud-free imagery existed up to two days before collapse.
- **"None of the datasets produced in this work could predict the timing of collapse."**
- The scale problem: the block covered 0.25 km²; the Himalaya spans ~0.5 million km². Manual expert review is "not feasible on a large scale."

**Verdict on satellite precursors: RETROSPECTIVE. They fail the OPERATIONAL test then and — because the failure is physical (no terminal acceleration), not instrumental — now. Better sensors do not fix the absence of a signal.**

**Seismic is the opposite story.**

**Tiwari et al., Scientific Reports 12:3733, 2022:** continuous high-frequency tremors (10–35 Hz) with a nucleation sequence extending ~**2.5 hours** before failure, amplitude rising sharply ~1 minute before detachment. Critically, **restricted to the nearest station, 12 km away.** Avoiding false alarms requires "a proper integrated network of highly sensitive hydrological, meteorological and seismic observatories."

**Cook et al., Science, 2021** (paywalled; via Petley's Landslide Blog, 4 Oct 2021): detection at stations up to 100 km; three resolvable phases; a 15-minute window between failure and impact at Tapovan. Operational warning requires network optimization, automated processing, a larger event catalog, dissemination and public education.

**Rawat et al., Earth Systems and Environment 8(1):85–104, 2024:** prospective warning windows of ~**10 minutes at 13 km** and ~**14 minutes at 19.7 km**. Explicitly retrospective; no operational system was running.

**Verdict on seismic: OPERATIONAL is achievable but was not achieved. The 2.5-hour nucleation phase does *not* meet the bar (visible only at 12 km; no comparable station near Langtang Lirung). The *collapse itself* meets it comfortably.**

## 8. Had anyone flagged this valley after July 2025?

**Yes. Unambiguously, repeatedly, and with specificity.**

**The July 2025 event:** on 8 July 2025, a supraglacial lake system atop the Purepu/Pyurepu Glacier — ~35–36 km upstream of Rasuwagadhi at ~5,150 m — drained rapidly. The lake expanded from 0.525 km² (28 June) to 0.725 km² (7 July); Kathmandu Post, citing ICIMOD (11 July 2025), gives 0.75 km² pre-flood shrinking to 0.61 km². The Nepal–China Friendship (Miteri) Bridge was destroyed, the Rasuwagadhi dry port severely damaged, a 16 km corridor severed. Seven hydropower projects lost nearly 250 MW — roughly 8% of national supply. Casualties reported variously as 9 dead/19 missing (Kathmandu Post), 11 killed/17 missing (Stimson), 18 missing/11 recovered (Nepalnews). **No warning was issued.** The lake was "previously unnoticed" and "unmonitored." Nepal then had **two functioning glacial-lake early warning systems against 21 lakes classified as potentially dangerous.**

**Published afterwards:**

- The **HiRISK scientific collective** (incl. Jakob Steiner, Amrit Thapa) identified the mechanism on **9 July 2025** — one day after.
- **Stimson Center (updated 22 July 2025):** the Purepu lakes had **not completely drained**; ~**15–20 million m³ may remain stored**; the system **could refill during monsoon and drain again**; other high-risk supraglacial lakes in proximate watersheds required urgent monitoring — naming Nyanang Phu in Nyalam County; "the broader Lhende watershed contains dozens of smaller glacial lakes posing unquantified risks." Recommended a site-based EWS built jointly with China, remote sensing blended with citizen science, cross-border data sharing.
- **ICIMOD** and Nepali agencies "had already flagged the Bhote Koshi and Lhende systems as high-risk zones warranting continuous monitoring" (Nepalnews).
- A Nepal–China data-sharing understanding followed the 2025 event. **It was not operational on 26 August 2026.** NDRRMA spokesperson Shanti Mahat: Chinese authorities claimed equal unawareness of onset. DHM hydrologist Sauharda Joshi: **"If we had been informed while the flood was building up in Tibet, human casualties could have been significantly minimized."**

**This is not a knowledge failure. It is an implementation failure with a thirteen-month lead time and a published warning naming the watershed.** Context: DHM's budget fell from Rs 765 million (FY 2078/79) to Rs 554 million (2080/81) to Rs 338 million (2081/82) — a 56% fall in three years (Spotlight, 28 Aug 2026).

**Reading of the clock.** The entire achievable gain from any realistic system is concentrated in the top 20 km, and it amounts to **roughly 20–40 minutes** at Rasuwagadhi, Timure and Syabrubesi — the difference between a negative lead time and a positive one. Below Betrawati, the human telephone chain already delivered hours, and no technology adds materially to it. **A seismic-plus-tripwire architecture is worth building for three settlements and worth almost nothing for the rest of the corridor.** Whether those three settlements account for the casualties is the unresolved recovery-location question from §1.

The **station-loss alarm requires no new hardware** and would have fired at roughly 08:53 — comparable to, and more reliable than, the human chain that actually fired at 09:00. It is the cheapest available intervention by a wide margin.

## 11. What to resolve before committing capital

1. **Where did people actually die?** 538 recorded deaths (as of 15:00 NPT 28 Aug) cluster in Chitwan (221) and Nawalparasi East (134) — districts with hours of warning; Rasuwa records 12. If recovery-location bias, the case for upper-valley warning is strong. If genuine mortality distribution, **the binding constraint is comprehension and reach, not lead time** — and the money belongs in sirens, multilingual alerting, tourist channels and highway control, not seismic discriminators.
2. **Sampling interval and telemetry architecture of the destroyed gauges** — determines flow-drop tripwire viability and whether a station-loss alarm can run on existing hardware.
3. **False-alarm rate for seismic landslide discrimination in the Nepal Himalaya.** No published figure. Without it the seismic pathway cannot be costed.
4. **Whether the 08:37 signal could have been classified as a landslide in under three minutes** with a purpose-built discriminator. USGS took hours and got it wrong first. The single technical question on which the seismic architecture stands or falls.
